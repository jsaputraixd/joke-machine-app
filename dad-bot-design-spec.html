Joke Machine — Design Spec v0.1

Dad-Bot

Visual and character design language for the joke machine's face. Companion document to the PRD — covers mood, palette, type, and the face state sheet used to build the animated character.

01 — Character Overview

Genuinely, earnestly convinced every joke is a banger. Delivers punchlines with theatrical pride, waits a beat too long for a reaction, and wins either way — groan or laugh. Dad mannerisms translated into robot behavior: finger-guns, a self-satisfied nod, an occasional "...get it?" on the worst ones.

02 — Mood & Aesthetic Direction

Two references collide on purpose: retro CRT / phosphor-terminal HUD (amber glow, scanline texture, monospace readouts — the "old tech" half of dad energy) crossed with Y2K grunge streetwear accents (a single hot-pink sticker-style pop, distressed edges, not polished or corporate). The face itself stays geometric and minimal — the personality comes from timing and micro-expression, not surface decoration.

Guardrail: one grunge/Y2K accent color max, used sparingly (sticker/glitch moments only) so the HUD language stays legible and doesn't turn into a costume.

03 — Color Palette

Void / BG#0B0D0A
Frame Amber#FFB020
Phosphor Green#7DFFA3
Cream#F4E9D8
Pop Accent#FF3EA5

Amber = the face's idle glow and frame. Phosphor green = active/talking state. Cream = body text and UI. Pop pink = punchline-only flourish (finger-guns glyph, "get it?" callout) — never used at rest.

04 — Typography

HUD / Display — Space Mono or IBM Plex Mono
TELL ME A JOKE_
Body — system sans / Inter
Why don't scientists trust atoms? Because they make up everything.

Monospace carries all HUD labels, buttons, and status text (reinforces the terminal identity). Body/joke text renders in a clean sans for readability — the joke itself should never fight the chrome around it.

05 — Face State Sheet

Construction: rounded-square HUD frame, two square "cube" eyes (nod to Animated-Robot-Face / Emo-style bots), a single pill-shaped mouth that changes height/width per state. Mustache glyph sits between eyes and mouth as the one dad signifier — present in every state, most exaggerated at the punchline.

01 · Idle
Slow blink loop, subtle "breathing" scale pulse on the frame glow. Signals presence without demanding attention between jokes.
02 · Thinking
Eyes narrow to half-height, three-dot loading glyph in the corner. Holds while the LLM/TTS call is in flight.
03 · Talking (open)
Wide mouth-open frame, driven by ElevenLabs vowel-timing data. Frame outline switches to phosphor green while audio is playing.
04 · Talking (closed)
Consonant/small-mouth frame — alternates with 03 during speech per character timing to produce the "talking" impression.
05 · Lean-in
Whole frame shifts forward/scales up ~4%, slight tilt, eyes narrow — the "wait for it" beat right before the punchline.
06 · Punchline reaction
Big grin, one-eye wink, finger-guns glyph flanking the frame, frame outline flashes pop pink. The payoff beat — worth the most animation polish.

06 — Dad Signifier

Single signifier chosen: a small pixel-mustache glyph, centered between the eyes and mouth. Present at low opacity in every state so the character reads as "dad" even at rest, and pulses to full opacity on the punchline-reaction beat. Deliberately not a full costume (no hat, no shirt pattern) — keeps the HUD-minimal language intact.

07 — Motion Notes

Idle: blink every 3–5s (randomized, not metronomic), frame glow pulses subtly — "alive," not static.

Talking: mouth state driven directly by ElevenLabs character-timing data (see PRD §5) — no separate animation timeline to hand-author.

Punchline: the single moment allowed to break the otherwise-restrained HUD language — this is where color, motion, and the mustache flourish all peak together. Everywhere else, restraint.
